# Women Weavers Cooperative, Savannakhet

**Project Profile and 18-month Progress Note**

*Fictional example document*

Lead organization: Lao Craft Heritage Association
Partner: Savannakhet Provincial Women's Union; Savannakhet Department of Industry and Commerce
Donor: Artisan Livelihoods Fund
Country: Lao PDR
Location: Kaysone Phomvihane and Champhone districts, Savannakhet Province (11 villages)
Timeline: 1 February 2024 – 31 January 2027
Total budget: USD 240,000
Beneficiaries: 185 women weavers and their families (about 900 people)
Status: Ongoing
Project coordinator: Souphaphone Keomany
SDGs: SDG 5: Gender Equality; SDG 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth; SDG 1: No Poverty

## Purpose

Support women weavers to form a cooperative, improve product quality and design, and sell traditional silk and cotton textiles to fair-trade and export buyers at a fair price, instead of selling individually to middlemen.

## Context

Savannakhet has a long tradition of silk weaving, but most weavers work alone at home and sell to traders who set the price. A baseline study in 2023 found that weavers earned on average USD 55 per month from textiles, that 70% had no savings, and that younger women were leaving weaving for factory jobs in Thailand.

## Progress in the first 18 months

### Cooperative

- 185 weavers from 11 villages joined the cooperative (target 200 by project end).
- The cooperative was formally registered with the Department of Industry and Commerce in October 2024 and elected a board of 7 members.
- Members contribute LAK 50,000 per month to a savings fund; the fund has LAK 190 million and has given 41 small loans for looms and raw silk.

### Quality and production

- A shared dyeing and finishing workshop opened in Kaysone Phomvihane in June 2024.
- 42 weavers trained in natural dyeing (indigo, lac, ebony, teak leaves).
- 60 weavers trained in quality standards; rejected pieces fell from 22% to 6%.
- A young designer from Vientiane worked with the cooperative for three months on contemporary designs that keep traditional motifs.

### Sales

- First export order (1,200 scarves) shipped to a fair-trade buyer in Japan in March 2025; second order (2,000 pieces) in progress.
- Sales through a shop in Savannakhet town and at trade fairs in Vientiane: USD 48,000 in 2025.
- Average weaver income from textiles increased by 35% to about USD 74 per month; the top third of weavers earn over USD 120.

### Social

- A childcare corner at the workshop allows mothers with small children to attend training.
- 14 young women (under 25) have joined the cooperative as weavers, reversing the trend of leaving the craft.

## Budget status

Spent to date: USD 128,000 (53%).

## Challenges

- Raw silk prices rose 20% in 2025; the cooperative is testing its own mulberry and silkworm production with 12 households.
- Export paperwork is complex; the Department of Industry and Commerce assigned an officer to assist.
- Some husbands initially objected to women travelling to trade fairs; family meetings organised by the Women's Union helped.

## Next steps

- Launch an online shop (in Lao, English and Japanese) by early 2026.
- Register a collective trademark "Savan Silk".
- Train 20 members in bookkeeping and cooperative governance so that the board can run without project staff after January 2027.
