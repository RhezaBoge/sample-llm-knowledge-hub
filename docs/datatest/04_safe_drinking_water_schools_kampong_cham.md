# Safe Drinking Water for Primary Schools, Kampong Cham

**Project Completion Report**

*Fictional example document*

Implementing organization: Clear Water Cambodia
Partners: Kampong Cham Provincial Office of Education, Youth and Sport; Provincial Department of Rural Development
Donor: Mekong Rural Development Foundation
Country: Cambodia
Location: Kampong Siem, Prey Chhor and Cheung Prey districts, Kampong Cham Province
Start date: 1 March 2022

Total budget: USD 196,500 (final expenditure USD 194,100)
Beneficiaries: 9,600 students and 410 teachers in 29 schools

## 1. Background

A 2021 assessment by Clear Water Cambodia found that 71% of rural primary schools in the three target districts relied on untreated pond or shallow well water, and that one in four schools had no water source at all during the dry season. Children often brought no water from home, and teachers reported that stomach illness was among the most common reasons for absence.

## 2. Objective

Provide reliable, safe drinking water and hygiene education in 29 primary schools, with school-based committees able to maintain the systems after the project ends.

## 3. What was done

### 3.1 Water systems

- 58 bio-sand filters installed (two per school), each producing up to 60 litres per hour.
- 58 rainwater harvesting tanks (5,000 litres each) installed with first-flush diverters.
- 11 schools without any water source received a new drilled well with a hand pump.
- 29 drinking-water stations built with 6 taps each, at child height, next to hand-washing basins.

### 3.2 Committees and maintenance

- A water committee was formed in each school: the school director, two teachers, two parents and two student leaders.
- 29 caretakers were trained in filter cleaning, tank maintenance and basic repairs; each school received a maintenance kit and a laminated checklist in Khmer.
- Each committee opened a small maintenance fund with an initial USD 100 from the project and monthly parent contributions of KHR 500–1,000 per family.

### 3.3 Hygiene education

- "Clean Hands, Healthy School" lessons were delivered to 9,600 students over three school years.
- 410 teachers trained in delivering the lessons.
- Student hygiene clubs in all 29 schools run weekly hand-washing demonstrations.

## 4. Results

| Indicator | Baseline (2022) | End of project (2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Schools with safe drinking water available all day | 0 of 29 | 29 of 29 |
| Water quality tests meeting national standard (E. coli) | 12% of samples | 97% of samples |
| Absences reported as stomach illness | 11% of absences | 4% of absences |
| Students washing hands with soap before eating (observed) | 23% | 81% |
| Active water committees with a maintenance fund | 0 | 29 |

Water quality was tested every six months by the Provincial Department of Rural Development laboratory.

## 6. Lessons learned

1. Parent contributions to the maintenance fund were higher where the committee showed the fund balance on the school notice board every month.
2. Rainwater tanks alone were not enough in the dry season (February–April); the combination with filters and, where needed, a well, was essential.
3. Student hygiene clubs kept hand-washing rates high even in schools where teachers changed.

## 7. Sustainability and handover

All systems were handed over to the school committees in July 2025. The Provincial Office of Education has adopted the water-committee model and will fund it in 40 additional schools from its 2026 budget. Clear Water Cambodia will carry out one follow-up visit in early 2026 at its own cost.
