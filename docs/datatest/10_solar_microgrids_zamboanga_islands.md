# Solar Micro-grids for Island Communities, Zamboanga del Norte

**Final Project Report**

*Fictional example document*

Implementing organization: Sun Islands Energy Cooperative
Co-implementer: Zamboanga del Norte Provincial Government (Provincial Engineering Office)
Donor: Pacific Clean Energy Partnership
Country: Philippines
Location: Sibutad and Rizal municipalities, Zamboanga del Norte (four islands: Lipay, Minang, Sitio Balas and Bantayan Island)
Period: 1 October 2022 – 30 September 2025
Total budget: USD 1,150,000 (final expenditure USD 1,138,600)
Beneficiaries: 940 households (about 4,700 people), 6 schools, 4 health stations
Status: Completed
Project manager: Engr. Rodel Macapagal
SDGs: SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy; SDG 1: No Poverty; SDG 13: Climate Action

## 1. Summary

Four small islands with no connection to the national grid received solar micro-grids with battery storage. Each micro-grid is owned by a community energy cooperative that collects monthly fees, pays local technicians and sets aside a maintenance reserve. Electricity is now available 24 hours a day instead of about 4 hours of diesel generator power in the evening. Household energy spending fell by about 45%, and new small businesses started on all four islands.

## 2. Context

Before the project, the islands depended on community diesel generators running from 6 to 10 pm, with frequent breakdowns and fuel shortages, and on kerosene lamps. Fish could not be stored, children studied by lamplight, and the health stations could not refrigerate vaccines.

Each system includes a distribution network with prepaid smart meters, street lighting and a dedicated line for the school and health station.

## 4. Results

- 940 households, 6 schools and 4 health stations connected (100% of target).
- 24-hour electricity on all islands; average availability in the last 12 months: 98.6%.
- Average household energy spending fell from PHP 1,450 to PHP 790 per month.
- 16 local technicians certified by TESDA in solar system maintenance (4 per island, including 5 women).
- All four cooperatives are financially self-sustaining: fee collection rate 93%, and every cooperative has a maintenance reserve of at least three months of operating costs.
- Productive uses: 2 fish-drying facilities, 1 ice maker, 3 small shops with refrigeration, 1 rice mill and 14 home-based businesses (sewing, phone charging, food preparation).
- Vaccine refrigeration available at all 4 health stations; evening study hours for children increased from 1.1 to 2.6 hours (parent survey).
- Diesel consumption reduced by an estimated 38,000 litres per year, about 100 tonnes of CO2.

## 6. Lessons learned

1. Prepaid meters were essential for fee collection and avoided disputes.
2. Training women technicians improved maintenance response, since several male technicians are away fishing for days at a time.
3. Battery sizing for three cloudy days proved right; no island had a blackout longer than six hours.
4. The productive-use grants (USD 500–3,000 per business) turned electricity into income quickly and strengthened support for paying fees.

## 7. Sustainability

Each cooperative has a service agreement with a solar supplier in Dipolog City for major repairs. The Provincial Government plans to replicate the model on two more islands (Siocon and Sirawai municipalities) in 2026 with its own budget and the cooperatives' technicians as trainers.
