- SDGs: SDG 2: Zero Hunger; SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

## 2. Purpose of the programme

East Nusa Tenggara has one of the highest rates of child stunting in Indonesia. The programme helps families with young children grow vegetables and keep laying hens in their yards, and trains community health volunteers to teach simple, nutritious menus using the garden produce. Child growth is monitored at the monthly posyandu (community health post).

## 3. Progress report (October 2024 – July 2025)

Presented by the programme manager.

- 640 families have established a nutrition garden (64% of the two-year target) with seeds, seedlings, tools and a small chicken coop with 5 hens.
- 58 community health volunteers trained in nutrition, menu planning and garden support (target 80).
- 24 posyandu monitor the growth of 1,120 children under five every month.
- Families report eating vegetables 5.2 days per week on average, compared with 2.8 days at baseline.
- Egg production: about 2,100 eggs per week across participating families; 60% consumed at home, 40% sold.
- Among children monitored for at least 6 months, the share with low weight-for-age fell from 19% to 14%.
- Spent to date: IDR 1,210,000,000 (42%).

## 4. Discussion

**4.1 Water in the dry season.** Village heads from Amarasi reported that about a quarter of gardens suffered in the long dry season (May–October) because of water shortages. The district health office suggested simple water tanks and drip irrigation made from used bottles. *Decision:* provide 200 water tanks (1,000 litres) to the driest villages and train all families in bottle drip irrigation. Budget to be taken from the Year 2 contingency line (IDR 160 million).

**4.2 Chicken health.** Some families lost hens to Newcastle disease in March. *Decision:* organise vaccination with the regency livestock office twice a year; train volunteers in basic poultry care.

**4.3 Men's involvement.** Volunteers noted that fathers rarely attend posyandu sessions. *Decision:* hold two "fathers' cooking afternoons" per village in the next six months.

**4.4 Data.** The Health Offices asked for the programme's growth data in the national e-PPGBM format. *Decision:* programme staff will export monthly data in that format from September.
