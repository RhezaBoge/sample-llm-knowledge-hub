# Village Midwife Skills Programme, East Flores

**Project Outline and Year 1 Results**

*Fictional example document*

Implementing organization: Flores Health Partners
Partners: East Flores District Health Office; Larantuka District Hospital; Indonesian Midwives Association (IBI) East Flores branch
Donor: Island Health Foundation
Country: Indonesia
Location: Larantuka, Adonara and Solor islands, East Flores Regency, East Nusa Tenggara Province
Period: 1 August 2024 – 31 July 2027
Total budget: USD 275,000
Beneficiaries: 64 village midwives; about 2,350 pregnant women per year; 22 village health posts
Status: Ongoing
Programme manager: Dr. Maria Goreti Kedang
SDGs: SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being; SDG 5: Gender Equality

## 1. Purpose

Strengthen the skills, equipment and referral links of village midwives on three islands so that mothers can receive good antenatal care and safe deliveries close to home, with timely referral to the district hospital in Larantuka when needed.

## 2. Context

East Flores Regency consists of the eastern tip of Flores and the islands of Adonara and Solor. Many villages are several hours from the hospital by road and boat, and sea crossings are difficult in the rainy season. Village midwives are the first and often the only health worker a pregnant woman sees. A 2024 assessment found that half of the midwives had not received refresher training in more than five years and that 40% of village health posts lacked basic delivery equipment or reliable lighting.

## 3. Components

1. **Skills:** refresher training for all 64 village midwives in antenatal care, normal delivery, newborn care, recognising danger signs and emergency referral; followed by supervised practice at the district hospital.
2. **Equipment:** basic delivery kits, newborn resuscitation equipment, blood pressure monitors and solar lighting for 22 village health posts.
3. **Referral:** a radio-and-phone referral system linking the islands with the hospital, and agreements with boat owners for emergency transport.
4. **Community:** monthly "mother's classes" in 30 villages on nutrition, danger signs, birth planning and newborn care, led by midwives with support from village women's groups.
5. **Data:** a simple digital register for antenatal visits and deliveries, synchronised when there is a mobile signal.

## 4. Year 1 results (August 2024 – July 2025)

| Indicator | Baseline | Year 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Midwives with refresher training in the last 2 years | 31 of 64 | 64 of 64 |
| Village health posts with complete delivery kit and lighting | 13 of 22 | 22 of 22 |
| Pregnant women with at least 4 antenatal visits (per year) | 1,620 | 2,350 |
| Deliveries attended by a trained midwife | 71% | 88% |
| Emergency referrals reaching the hospital within 4 hours | 48% | 79% |
| Villages with a monthly mother's class | 0 | 30 |

- 64 midwives completed the 10-day refresher course and a 5-day hospital placement.
- 22 village health posts received equipment and solar lighting.
- The referral system handled 112 emergency referrals; boat owners in 9 coastal villages are on call.
- 2,350 pregnant women received at least four antenatal visits.
- 1,800 women attended mother's classes.

## 5. Budget

Spent in Year 1: USD 118,000 (43%). Equipment and solar lighting accounted for USD 61,000.

## 6. Lessons

- Hospital placements were the most valued part of training; midwives asked for them to be repeated yearly.
- Solar lighting had an unexpected effect: night deliveries at health posts increased because women felt safer there.
- Boat agreements work when the fee is agreed in advance and paid quickly by the district health office.

## 7. Plans for Year 2

- Train 20 additional midwives on Solor island who joined the service in 2025.
- Introduce the digital antenatal register in all 22 health posts.
- Add newborn hearing and vision screening to mother's classes with the hospital's paediatric department.
- Begin discussions with the District Health Office on taking over the referral system costs from 2027.
