Hi team,

Here are my notes from Tuesday's quarterly review so that everyone has the same picture. Please correct anything I got wrong by Friday, as I will send the summary to the council and to our funder.

**Programme basics (for the record)**

- Programme name: Neighbourhood Elderly Care Volunteers (NECV)
- Implementing organization: Silver Lining Community Society
- Partners: Penang Island City Council (MBPP); Penang State Health Department (two clinics in Air Itam); Universiti Sains Malaysia School of Medical Sciences (student volunteers)
- Funder: Northern Region Community Care Foundation
- Country: Malaysia
- Areas: George Town, Air Itam and Bayan Lepas, Penang Island
- Period: 1 April 2024 – 31 March 2027
- Total budget: MYR 1,620,000 over three years
- Programme coordinator: Lim Mei Ling (me); field supervisors: Rajan Pillai (George Town), Nurul Huda (Air Itam), Tan Wei Keong (Bayan Lepas)
- Status: ongoing, Year 2
- SDGs we report against: SDG 3 (health and well-being), SDG 11 (inclusive cities), SDG 10 (reduced inequalities)

**Why we do this**

Penang has the oldest population of any Malaysian state. In the three areas, about 1 in 5 residents over 65 lives alone. The programme trains neighbourhood volunteers to visit older residents regularly, help with small practical things, spot health or safety problems early, and reduce loneliness.

**Numbers this quarter (July–September 2025)**

- 210 trained volunteers active (the annual target was 180, so we are ahead)
- 1,460 older residents visited at least twice a month (target 1,200)
- 85 homes received grab bars, non-slip mats or better lighting through the small-repairs fund (MYR 42,000 spent)
- 12 "kopi mornings" held in community halls, average 60 participants each
- 37 residents referred to the clinics in Air Itam after volunteers noticed health concerns (mostly blood pressure and falls)
- 9 residents connected with the state welfare department for financial aid

Cumulative since April 2024: 2,900 residents reached, 4,100 home visits per month on average, 168 home safety repairs.

**What people told us**

The regular visit is what matters most, especially for residents living alone. Several residents said they now "have someone who notices if I am not well". Volunteers asked for a short first-aid refresher, which we will schedule in November. Volunteers in Bayan Lepas find transport difficult because the area is spread out; Wei Keong suggested a small transport allowance for volunteers who visit more than 6 residents.

**Finance**

Spent to date: MYR 690,000 (43% of total budget, 50% of time elapsed), so we are a little under-spent. The main reason is that the small-repairs fund started late. We expect to catch up in Q4.

**Issues**

1. Volunteer retention in George Town is lower (72%) than in the other two areas (85–90%). Rajan thinks it is because many George Town volunteers are students who leave after graduation. We will recruit more residents from the neighbourhoods themselves.
2. The referral form for the clinics is still on paper. The Health Department has agreed to try a simple online form from January.
3. We need a clearer policy on volunteers handling residents' money (for example, buying groceries). Nurul will draft one.

**Decisions**

- Start the clinic referral pilot in Air Itam in January 2026 (both clinics).
- Add a transport allowance of MYR 50/month for volunteers with more than 6 residents (from Q4).
- Hold the first-aid refresher on 15 November.
- Prepare the Year 2 report for the council by 31 October.

**Next quarterly review:** 13 January 2026, MBPP community hall, Air Itam.

Thanks everyone – it has been a good quarter.
