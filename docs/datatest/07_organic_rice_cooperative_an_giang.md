# Organic Rice Farmer Cooperative, An Giang

**Annual Progress Report 2025**

*Fictional example document*

**Implementing organizations:** Mekong Farmers Alliance (lead); An Giang Agricultural Extension Centre
**Donor:** Green Fields Foundation
**Co-funding:** An Giang Provincial People's Committee (land for the storage facility)
**Country:** Viet Nam
**Location:** Tri Ton and Thoai Son districts, An Giang Province
**Project period:** 1 June 2023 – 31 May 2026
**Total budget:** USD 362,000
**Beneficiaries:** 310 farming households (about 1,300 people) in 2025
**Status:** Ongoing – final year
**Project director:** Nguyen Thi Hong Van
**SDGs:** SDG 2: Zero Hunger; SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production; SDG 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth

## 1. Objective

Help smallholder rice farmers in An Giang switch to organic production, obtain certification and sell at a better price through a cooperative they own.

## 2. Progress in 2025

### 2.1 Membership and area

| Indicator | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Target 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Member households | 95 | 180 | 310 | 350 |
| Hectares under organic management | 110 | 220 | 410 | 450 |
| Households with organic certification | 0 | 0 | 142 | 300 |
| Women-headed member households | 18 | 39 | 71 | – |

### 2.2 Production and sales

- Average yield on organic plots: 5.1 tonnes per hectare per season, compared with 5.9 tonnes for conventional plots nearby. Lower yields are offset by lower input costs (no chemical fertiliser or pesticides) and higher prices.
- Average selling price: 19% above conventional rice (8% in 2024).
- The cooperative signed its first supply contract with a supermarket chain in Can Tho (240 tonnes per year) and a second with an exporter in Ho Chi Minh City (400 tonnes, certification required).
- Net income per hectare per season for certified members: VND 21.4 million, compared with VND 15.8 million for conventional farmers in the baseline survey.

### 2.3 Infrastructure

- A shared drying and storage facility (capacity 600 tonnes) opened in Tri Ton in July 2025, on land provided by the province.
- A small rice-milling line was installed in November 2025, allowing the cooperative to sell packaged rice under its own brand "Bảy Núi Organic".

### 2.4 Training

- 14 farmer field schools ran through the wet season (natural pest management, water-saving irrigation, record keeping for certification).
- 46 lead farmers trained as peer trainers.
- 2 young staff hired by the cooperative to help older members with certification paperwork.

### 2.5 Environment

- Soil organic matter on plots in their third organic season increased from 1.9% to 2.6%.
- Water use fell by about 25% through alternate wetting and drying.

## 3. Budget

Spent to date: USD 281,000 (78%). The storage facility cost USD 12,000 more than budgeted because of steel price increases; this was covered by savings on training.

## 4. Challenges

- Certification paperwork is difficult for older farmers; the two new staff have reduced rejected applications from 30% to 8%.
- Buyers require consistent quality; the milling line and a quality-control checklist were introduced in response.
- Some neighbouring conventional farmers worried about pest spread; joint field days have reduced tension.

## 5. Lessons learned

The first supply contract was the turning point for membership: 90 households joined within two months of the Can Tho contract being announced. Secure markets drive adoption more than training alone.

## 6. Plan for 2026 (final year)

- Reach 300 certified households and 450 hectares.
- Transfer full management of the facility and brand to the cooperative board.
- Final evaluation in April 2026; exit workshop with the province in May 2026.
