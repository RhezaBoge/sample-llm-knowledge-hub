# Youth Heritage Guides, Luang Prabang

**Project Summary – Year 1 (November 2024 – October 2025)**

*Fictional example document*

- **Implementing organization:** Lao Heritage Youth Network
- **Partners:** Luang Prabang World Heritage Office; Luang Prabang Provincial Department of Information, Culture and Tourism; Ban Xang Khong and Ban Phanom village committees
- **Donor:** Culture and Livelihoods Fund
- **Country:** Lao PDR
- **Location:** Luang Prabang town and the nearby villages of Ban Xang Khong and Ban Phanom, Luang Prabang Province
- **Period:** 1 November 2024 – 31 October 2026
- **Total budget:** USD 165,000
- **Beneficiaries:** 40 young guides; 2 craft villages (about 180 artisan households); owners of 2 restored houses
- **Status:** Ongoing
- **Project coordinator:** Bounmy Phommachanh
- **SDGs:** SDG 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth; SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities; SDG 4: Quality Education

## 1. Description

Young people from Luang Prabang and nearby villages are trained and licensed as heritage guides. They offer walking and cycling tours that explain the town's architecture, Buddhist traditions, crafts and food culture, and a craft trail to the weaving and paper-making villages. A share of tour income goes into a fund for restoring small traditional wooden houses, which are disappearing as owners replace them with concrete buildings.

## 2. Context

Luang Prabang has been a UNESCO World Heritage town since 1995. Tourism recovered strongly after 2023, but most guiding jobs went to people from outside the province, and young residents saw few benefits. At the same time, the World Heritage Office recorded the loss of 40 traditional houses between 2019 and 2024.

## 3. Results in Year 1

### Guides

- 40 young people (24 women, 16 men, aged 18–27) completed a 12-week course (heritage knowledge, guiding skills, English, first aid, responsible tourism) and obtained the provincial guide licence.
- Guides earn an average of USD 160 per month in the high season (November–March) and USD 70 in the low season; before the project most were unemployed or in informal work.
- 6 guides have been hired by hotels and tour companies in addition to running their own tours.

### Tours

- 1,900 visitors joined the tours (walking tour 1,100; cycling tour 450; craft trail 350).
- Average visitor rating on the booking platform: 4.8 out of 5.
- Tours are offered in Lao, English and French; 4 guides are learning Chinese.

### Craft trail and villages

- The craft trail connects 14 weaving and paper-making workshops in Ban Xang Khong and Ban Phanom, with signage, a map and a small visitor centre in each village.
- Artisans report an average 25% increase in direct sales to visitors.

### House restoration

- The restoration fund received 15% of tour income (USD 4,100) plus a matching donor contribution.
- 2 traditional wooden houses restored with their owners (one in Ban Xieng Mouane, one in Ban Phanom); 3 more assessed for 2026.
- 8 young carpenters trained in traditional joinery during the restorations.

## 4. Budget

Spent to date: USD 82,000 (50%).

## 5. Lessons

- Guides who grew up in the craft villages give the most popular tours; personal stories matter more than facts.
- Tourists are willing to pay a small premium when they know part of the price funds restoration; the booking page explains this.
- Guides asked for additional language training in Chinese and Korean, which is planned for Year 2.

## 6. Year 2 plans

- Train 30 more guides, including 10 from Chomphet district across the river.
- Add a "Mekong boat and village" tour.
- Restore 3 more houses; publish a short guide for owners on maintaining traditional houses.
- Hand the booking platform and the restoration fund to a guides' association registered under Lao law.
