# Inclusive Community Sports Programme, Johor Bahru

**Year 1 Report (March 2025 – February 2026)**

*Fictional example document*

- **Organization:** Play Together Malaysia
- **Partners:** Johor Bahru City Council; Johor State Education Department; 12 public schools; Johor Paralympic Sports Association
- **Donor:** ASEAN Sports for Development Fund
- **Country:** Malaysia
- **Location:** Johor Bahru and Iskandar Puteri, Johor
- **Dates:** 1 March 2025 – 28 February 2028
- **Total budget:** MYR 2,100,000
- **Beneficiaries (Year 1):** 1,850 children aged 7–15 (310 with disabilities); 36 coaches; 12 schools
- **Status:** Ongoing
- **Programme director:** Farah Aziz
- **SDGs:** SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being; SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities; SDG 4: Quality Education

## 1. Goal

Make community sport accessible to children with and without disabilities by adapting school sports facilities, training coaches in inclusive methods and running weekly mixed-ability sessions in football, athletics and swimming.

## 2. Background

A 2024 survey in Johor Bahru found that fewer than 10% of children with disabilities took part in any organised sport, compared with 55% of other children. Reasons given by parents were lack of adapted facilities, coaches who did not know how to include their children, and fear of bullying.

## 3. Year 1 results

### Coaches and schools

- 36 coaches (24 school PE teachers, 12 community coaches) completed a 40-hour inclusive coaching course developed with the Johor Paralympic Sports Association.
- 12 schools run weekly mixed-ability sessions (football, athletics, swimming); 2 sessions per week per school.
- 4 school fields and 2 swimming pools adapted with ramps, tactile markings, accessible changing rooms and pool hoists.

### Participation

| Indicator | Year 1 result | Year 1 target |
|---|---|---|
| Children participating regularly | 1,850 | 1,500 |
| Of whom children with disabilities | 310 | 250 |
| Girls | 46% | 40% |
| Average weekly attendance | 78% | 70% |

### Events

- The first "Johor Inclusive Games" took place in November 2025 with 600 young athletes from 20 schools (including 8 non-project schools).
- 3 "family sports days" reached 1,400 parents and siblings.

### Outcomes

- Physical fitness tests (20-metre shuttle run, adapted for each child) improved for 71% of participants.
- Parents of children with disabilities reported better confidence and more friendships (survey of 180 parents: 84% agree).
- Teachers reported fewer incidents of bullying in participating classes (qualitative interviews, 10 schools).

## 4. Budget

Spent in Year 1: MYR 790,000 (38%). Facility adaptation was the largest cost (MYR 410,000).

## 5. Feedback and lessons

- Parents reported that children who had never joined sport before now attend regularly.
- Coaches asked for more adapted equipment (sound balls, sitting volleyball nets, racing wheelchairs), which is budgeted for Year 2.
- Pairing each child with a disability with a "sports buddy" worked well in swimming and athletics but less so in football, where the game moves faster. Small-sided games (5-a-side) were introduced.
- Transport remains a barrier for some children; a shuttle from two special-education centres started in January 2026.

## 6. Year 2 plans

- Add 8 schools (target 20), including 2 in Pasir Gudang.
- Train 24 more coaches and 40 student "sports buddies".
- Add two new sports: boccia and sitting volleyball.
- Second Johor Inclusive Games in November 2026, with the State Education Department as co-host.
