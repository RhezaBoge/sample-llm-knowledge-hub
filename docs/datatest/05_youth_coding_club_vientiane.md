# Youth Coding Clubs Vientiane

**Full Project Proposal**

*Fictional example document*

**Applicant:** Lao Digital Futures Association (LDFA)
**Co-applicant:** Vientiane Capital Education and Sports Department
**Proposed donor:** ASEAN Youth Skills Initiative
**Country:** Lao PDR
**Location:** Vientiane Capital – Chanthabouly, Sisattanak, Xaysetha and Sikhottabong districts
**Proposed period:** 1 September 2026 – 31 August 2028 (24 months)
**Requested budget:** USD 148,000 (total project cost USD 176,000; LDFA and partners contribute USD 28,000 in kind)
**Target group:** 1,350 secondary school students aged 13–18; 27 teachers
**Status:** Proposal submitted, awaiting decision
**Contact person:** Khamphet Vongsa, Executive Director
**SDGs:** SDG 4: Quality Education; SDG 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth; SDG 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

## 1. Problem statement

Demand for digital skills in Lao PDR is growing quickly, but secondary school students in Vientiane have few opportunities to learn programming. Private coding courses cost USD 150–300 per term, far beyond most families. A 2025 survey by LDFA in 12 public secondary schools found that 84% of students were interested in learning to code, 9% had ever written a programme, and no school had a functioning computer club. Teachers reported strong interest but lacked equipment, a curriculum and confidence.

## 2. Project goal

Give public secondary school students in Vientiane a free, high-quality introduction to programming through after-school clubs that continue after the project ends.

## 3. Proposed activities

### Component 1 – Set up 9 coding clubs

- Select 9 public secondary schools (at least 2 per district) with a committed school director and a secure room.
- Equip each club with 15 refurbished laptops, a projector, a Wi-Fi router and a 12-month internet subscription.
- Open each club for two 90-minute sessions per week after school.

### Component 2 – Train 27 teacher mentors

- Train 3 teachers per school in a 5-day course (Scratch, then Python basics, then project-based teaching).
- Provide monthly online coaching sessions and a mentor community chat group.
- Pay a modest mentor stipend of USD 25 per month.

### Component 3 – Curriculum

- Adapt an open curriculum into Lao: Level 1 "Create with Scratch" (12 weeks), Level 2 "Python for Problem Solvers" (12 weeks), Level 3 "Build an App for Your Community" (12 weeks).
- All materials published under an open licence on the LDFA website.

### Component 4 – Vientiane Youth App Fair

- A yearly fair where clubs present apps that solve a problem in their school or neighbourhood (for example, a canteen ordering app, a school library catalogue, a bus timetable).
- Jury of teachers, parents, local technology companies and the Education Department.

### Component 5 – Pathways

- Connect Level 3 graduates with internships at 6 technology companies in Vientiane that have expressed interest.
- Information sessions on scholarships and university courses in computer science.

## 6. Sustainability

- Clubs are owned by the schools; equipment is transferred to the school inventory at the end of the project.
- Mentors continue the clubs as part of their regular teaching hours, agreed in a memorandum with the Education Department.
- The open curriculum lets other schools start clubs without LDFA.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Teacher mentors transfer to other schools | Train 3 per school; mentor pool shared across schools |
| Internet connection too slow in some schools | Offline-first curriculum; local copies of all materials |
| Low participation of girls | Girls-only sessions in the first term; female mentors in each club |

## 8. Monitoring

Attendance records per session, pre/post skills tests per level, a participant survey each term, and an external review in month 20.
