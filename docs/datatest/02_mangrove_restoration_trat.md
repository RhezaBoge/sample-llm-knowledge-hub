# Mangrove Restoration and Blue Carbon Learning, Trat Province

**Mid-term Review Report**

*Fictional example document*

- **Implementing organization:** Gulf Coast Conservation Network (GCCN)
- **Local partners:** Laem Klat Sub-district Administrative Organization; Ban Nam Chiao Fishing Community Group; Trat Marine and Coastal Resources Office
- **Donor:** Southeast Asia Climate Action Fund
- **Country:** Thailand
- **Project sites:** Laem Klat and Nam Chiao sub-districts, Mueang Trat District, Trat Province
- **Duration:** 1 January 2023 – 31 December 2026
- **Total budget:** USD 410,000
- **Beneficiaries:** 1,850 people in 7 coastal villages (direct); about 6,000 people (indirect)
- **Status:** Ongoing
- **Reviewed by:** Independent review team, March 2025
- **SDGs:** SDG 13: Climate Action; SDG 14: Life Below Water; SDG 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth

## Executive summary

The project restores degraded mangrove areas along the Trat coast, builds local capacity to monitor mangrove growth and carbon storage, and links restoration to the income of fishing households. At mid-term, 42 of the planned 60 hectares have been replanted with a 78% survival rate, a community nursery produces 25,000 seedlings a year, and 36 youth monitors collect data that is published openly. Fishers report higher crab and shellfish catches near restored areas. The review team rates progress as satisfactory and recommends extending the monitoring approach to two neighbouring sub-districts in the second half of the project.

## 1. Context

Trat Province has one of the largest remaining mangrove areas on the eastern Gulf of Thailand. Between 1990 and 2015 about 30% of the mangroves in Laem Klat were converted to shrimp ponds, many of which were later abandoned. Abandoned ponds erode quickly, and villagers reported losing fishing grounds and protection from storm surges.

## 2. Project design

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| 1. Restoration | Replant 60 ha of abandoned shrimp ponds and eroded shoreline with native species (Rhizophora, Avicennia, Sonneratia) chosen by site conditions |
| 2. Community nursery | A nursery managed by the Ban Nam Chiao women's group, selling seedlings to other restoration projects |
| 3. Monitoring and learning | Train youth and fishers to measure tree growth, survival and carbon storage with simple tools and a mobile app |
| 4. Livelihoods | Support mangrove-friendly income: crab banks, mangrove honey, community tourism |

## 3. Results at mid-term

### Restoration

- 42 ha replanted (70% of target); 118,000 seedlings planted.
- Survival rate 78% overall; 91% on sites selected after the 2023 mapping exercise, 54% on the first two plots.
- 1.6 km of bamboo fencing installed to reduce wave energy on eroded shoreline.

### Community nursery

- Nursery capacity: 25,000 seedlings per year.
- Income from seedling sales to other projects: THB 180,000 in 2024, shared among 22 women.

### Monitoring

- 36 youth monitors trained (19 women, 17 men), from 4 schools and 2 fishing groups.
- 24 permanent monitoring plots established; data collected every three months.
- Estimated carbon stock in restored areas: 310 tonnes CO2e so far, calculated with the Thai Department of Marine and Coastal Resources methodology.
- Data published on the provincial open-data page and used by the Marine Office in its annual report.

### Livelihoods

- 3 crab banks established; participating fishers report a 20% increase in blue swimming crab catch compared with 2022.
- 14 households started mangrove beekeeping; first honey harvest of 260 kg sold at the Trat weekend market.
- A community kayak tour run by 8 young guides hosted 1,100 visitors in 2024.

## 4. Budget status

Spent to date: USD 219,000 (53%). Spending is slightly behind the time elapsed (54%) because the second nursery expansion was postponed to 2025. No major reallocations are needed.

## 5. Findings of the review team

1. **Site selection is the key success factor.** Early plots in low-lying mud with poor drainage had the lowest survival. The participatory mapping with fishers in mid-2023 corrected this, and later plots are doing well.
2. **Youth monitoring is credible.** A sample of 6 plots re-measured by the review team matched the youth data within 5%.
3. **Livelihood links are strong but small.** The crab banks and honey business reach 14–40 households each. Scaling up would need a market study.
4. **Gender:** women lead the nursery and honey groups; men dominate the crab banks. The project should document this balance in its final report.

## 6. Recommendations

- Extend youth monitoring to Huai Raeng and Ao Yai sub-districts in 2026, using the trained monitors as trainers.
- Prepare a simple "blue carbon learning kit" for schools in Thai and English.
- Commission a market study for mangrove honey and community tourism before investing further.
- Agree with the Marine Office on who maintains the monitoring plots after December 2026.

## 7. Lessons learned

Planting in the right zone matters more than planting many seedlings. Also, publishing data openly built trust with the provincial authorities faster than formal meetings did.
