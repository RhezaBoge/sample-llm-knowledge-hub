## 1. Summary

The project trains young carpenters and builders in treated, engineered bamboo construction and builds demonstration houses and community buildings that are affordable, low-carbon and designed to withstand typhoon winds. It also creates a local supply chain, from bamboo farmers to a treatment facility to construction enterprises.

## 2. Why bamboo

Bohol was hit by Typhoon Odette in 2021 and by a strong earthquake in 2013. Many families rebuilt with light materials that failed again. Bamboo grows abundantly on the island, matures in 3–5 years, stores carbon and, when properly treated and engineered, is strong, durable and resistant to wind. However, few builders know modern bamboo techniques, and untreated bamboo is seen as a "poor man's material".

## 3. Activities and progress (to October 2025)

### Training

- A 6-month course was developed with BISU, covering bamboo treatment, joinery, engineered panels, wind-resistant design and site safety. The course is accredited by TESDA.
- 96 trainees enrolled in two cohorts (target 150 over the project); 71 have graduated so far. 31% of trainees are women.
- 12 trainers from BISU and local construction companies certified.

The houses cost about 30% less than an equivalent concrete-block house and were tested for wind loads by BISU engineers.

### Supply chain

- A bamboo treatment facility (boron-based, vertical soak diffusion) opened in Carmen in April 2025, supplied by 40 local bamboo farmers who received training in harvesting and received a guaranteed price.
- 2 small construction enterprises formed by graduates; both have won municipal contracts (a school canteen and a market shed).

## 4. Budget

Spent to date: USD 171,000 (44%).

## 5. Challenges

- Building permits: municipal engineering offices had no reference standards for bamboo. The project worked with the Department of Public Works and Highways regional office to use the Philippine Bamboo Building Code draft; a simplified permit checklist is now in use in Loboc and Carmen.
- Public perception: open-house days at the demonstration buildings have been the most effective way to change views; 2,300 visitors so far.
- Some bamboo farmers harvested too young; a harvesting calendar and tagging system solved this.

## 6. Plans

- Enrol the third and fourth cohorts (54 trainees) in 2026.
- Complete 3 more demonstration buildings, including a classroom.
- Publish an open building manual in Cebuano and English by mid-2026.
- Work with the Tagbilaran City Engineering Office on a simplified permit process for bamboo houses, and with a housing finance cooperative on small loans for bamboo home construction.
