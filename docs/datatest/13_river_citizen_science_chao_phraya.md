## 1. About the project

Residents, students, boat operators and riverside temples collect simple water-quality readings and litter counts at 30 fixed points along the Chao Phraya every month. Results are published on an open online map and shared with district offices, which use them to plan litter traps, drain maintenance and clean-up events.

## 2. Activities in Year 1

### Training

- 420 citizen scientists trained in 18 workshops (students 55%, residents 30%, boat operators and temple volunteers 15%).
- Each monitoring team received a kit: turbidity tube, dissolved-oxygen test strips, pH strips, a litter-count sheet and a mobile app for uploading data with photos.
- 28 teachers from 14 schools trained to supervise school teams.

### Monitoring

- 2,900 water samples recorded at 30 points (turbidity, dissolved oxygen, pH, temperature).
- 1,850 litter counts recorded; plastic bags, foam food boxes and plastic bottles are the three most common items.
- Data quality: 8% of samples flagged by the university team for re-checking; teams with flagged data received a refresher session.

### Clean-ups and action

- 38 community clean-up events with 3,400 participants removed 61 tonnes of litter from riverbanks and canals.
- 3 district offices (Bang Sue, Dusit, Pak Kret) now use the open map to decide where to place litter traps; 12 new traps installed.
- Two temples installed waste-sorting stations for festival days after their own data showed litter peaks.

### Communication

- The open map (riverwatch-bkk.org, fictional) had 41,000 visits.
- 6 short videos produced by student teams; the most viewed had 180,000 views.

## 3. What the data shows

- Dissolved oxygen is lowest at points downstream of large canal outlets in Thon Buri, especially in the hot season.
- Litter counts fall by 30–50% within two months of a litter trap being installed nearby.
- School-adjacent points show the most regular sampling (95% of months) and the most reliable data.

## 4. Budget

Spent in Year 1: THB 4,600,000 (47%).

## 5. Lessons learned

- School groups keep the most regular schedule, and the open map motivates them because they can see their own data appear.
- Simple test strips are accurate enough for trends; the project does not try to replace the official laboratory monitoring.
- District offices respond faster to a map than to a report.

## 6. Plans for Year 2

- Add 10 monitoring points in Samut Prakan, downstream of Bangkok.
- Publish a short course for teachers on using river data in science classes.
- Hold a "River Data Day" with the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration in March 2026.
- Prepare a handover plan so that schools and the university continue monitoring after June 2026.
