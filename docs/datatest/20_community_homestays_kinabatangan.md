## 1. Rationale

The Lower Kinabatangan river attracts about 60,000 visitors a year who come to see orangutans, proboscis monkeys, pygmy elephants and hornbills. Most visitor spending goes to lodges owned by companies outside the district. Village homestays exist in Sukau, Bilit and Abai but receive only about 6% of visitors, because they lack training, a reliable booking system and agreed quality standards. Meanwhile, riverbank forest continues to be lost to agriculture, which reduces the wildlife that visitors come to see.

## 2. Project goal

Increase village income from tourism in the Lower Kinabatangan while restoring riverbank forest, so that communities have a direct economic reason to protect wildlife habitat.

## 3. Proposed activities

### Component 1 – Homestay quality

- Train 60 homestay families in hospitality, food safety, basic English and bookkeeping (5-day course plus follow-up visits).
- Support small upgrades (bathrooms, mosquito screens, solar water heaters) through matching grants of up to MYR 4,000 per family.
- Introduce a shared quality standard and a yearly inspection by the Sabah Tourism Board's homestay programme.

### Component 2 – Booking and marketing

- Set up a shared online booking platform run by a joint cooperative of the three villages, with a transparent price list and fair distribution of guests between families.
- Produce marketing materials and work with 10 responsible tour operators in Kota Kinabalu and Sandakan.

### Component 3 – Nature guides

- Train 30 local nature guides (at least 12 women) and support them to obtain the Sabah nature guide licence.
- Develop a code of conduct for responsible wildlife viewing (distance from animals, boat speed, no feeding) with the Sabah Wildlife Department.

### Component 4 – Riverbank forest corridor

- Plant 20 hectares of riverbank forest corridor with native species, with guests and villagers planting together ("plant a tree on your visit").
- Establish a community nursery in Bilit producing 30,000 seedlings per year.
- Monitor wildlife use of the corridor with camera traps managed by the guides.

## 6. Sustainability

The cooperative keeps 8% of each booking to pay for platform maintenance, inspections and the nursery. By year three this covers all recurring costs. The Sabah Tourism Board has agreed to include the three villages in its homestay promotion.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Drop in tourism (health or economic shocks) | Medium | Diversify to domestic visitors and school groups |
| Conflict between families over guest allocation | Medium | Transparent rotation rule in the platform; cooperative board handles complaints |
| Flooding of riverbank plots | High in wet season | Plant flood-tolerant species; plant after the wet season |
| Wildlife disturbance from more visitors | Medium | Code of conduct, boat speed limits, guide training |

## 8. Monitoring and evaluation

Baseline survey in month 2; yearly household income and occupancy surveys; corridor survival counts every six months; camera-trap reports; external evaluation in month 34.
