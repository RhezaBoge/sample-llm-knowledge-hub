**Annual Report – Year 1 (January – December 2025)**

## Summary

The programme sets up "waste banks" in primary and junior secondary schools in Makassar. Students sort plastic, paper and metal waste, deposit it in the school waste bank, and the school sells the material to partner recyclers. Savings are recorded in each student's passbook and used for school activities chosen by the students. The programme also introduces composting of organic waste from school canteens and integrates environmental education into the curriculum.

In Year 1, 32 schools established active waste banks, 48 tonnes of sorted waste were collected, and 19 schools started composting. The city Education Office has asked the programme to expand to all five remaining districts in 2026.

## 1. Why school waste banks

Makassar produces about 1,100 tonnes of waste per day, and less than 10% is recycled. The city's "Makassar Recover" plan calls for waste reduction at source. Schools are a good starting point: they produce a steady stream of clean, sorted waste, and children carry the sorting habit home.

## 2. Activities in Year 1

### 2.1 Setting up waste banks

- 32 schools selected (24 primary, 8 junior secondary) across five districts.
- Each school received sorting bins, a weighing scale, a storage cabinet, passbooks for students and a simple record book.
- A waste bank committee of 2 teachers and 6 students was formed in every school.

### 2.2 Training

- 120 teachers attended a two-day environmental education training.
- 256 student committee members were trained as "waste bank tellers".
- 4 waste bank managers from a community waste bank in Tamalanrea acted as mentors to the schools.

### 2.3 Collection and sales

| Material | Collected (tonnes) | Sold to | Income (IDR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plastic bottles and cups | 21.5 | PT Daur Ulang Celebes | 64,500,000 |
| Paper and cardboard | 19.2 | Makassar Paper Cooperative | 28,800,000 |
| Metal cans | 4.1 | local scrap dealers | 20,500,000 |
| Other (glass, cartons) | 3.2 | various | 4,100,000 |
| **Total** | **48.0** | | **117,900,000** |

Income is held in each school's waste bank account. Students voted to spend the savings on, among other things, a reading corner, sports equipment and a class trip to the Lakkang island mangrove.

### 2.4 Composting

- 19 schools built compost units for canteen waste; 6.8 tonnes of organic waste were composted.
- Compost is used in school gardens; 11 schools now grow vegetables for the canteen.

### 2.5 Curriculum

Environmental education modules (4 lessons per semester) were adopted in all 32 schools. Lessons cover waste sorting, the plastic cycle, composting and the city's waste system.

## 4. Budget

Spent in Year 1: IDR 1,380,000,000 (33% of total budget). The largest items were school equipment (IDR 520 million) and training (IDR 310 million).

## 5. Challenges

- Prices for recycled plastic fell by about 15% in the second half of 2025, reducing income per kilogram. The programme is negotiating a fixed-price agreement with the main buyer.
- Some schools lack storage space; waste must be collected weekly rather than monthly.
- Teachers asked for simpler record-keeping. A mobile app for recording deposits will be piloted in 2026.

## 6. Plans for 2026

- Add 30 schools in the districts of Mariso, Mamajang, Makassar, Ujung Pandang and Wajo.
- Pilot the mobile app in 10 schools.
- Hold the first "Clean School Competition" between districts, with the city mayor's office.
- Start a parents' waste bank in 5 neighbourhoods near participating schools.
