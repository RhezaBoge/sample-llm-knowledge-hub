# Floating Classrooms, Tonle Sap

**Concept Note**

*Fictional example document*

**Organization:** Lake Learning Cambodia
**Partners:** Pursat and Siem Reap Provincial Offices of Education; Kampong Luong and Chong Kneas commune councils
**Potential funder:** Open Horizons Education Fund
**Country:** Cambodia
**Location:** Kampong Luong floating village (Krakor district, Pursat Province) and Chong Kneas floating village (Siem Reap Province), Tonle Sap Lake
**Proposed duration:** 1 January 2027 – 31 December 2029 (36 months)
**Estimated budget:** USD 320,000
**Target group:** 360 children aged 6–12 and their families; 9 teachers
**Status:** Concept stage – seeking funding
**Contact:** Chea Sophal, Director
**SDGs:** SDG 4: Quality Education; SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities

## 1. The problem

About 1.2 million people live around the Tonle Sap, Southeast Asia's largest freshwater lake. Families in floating villages move several times a year as the water level rises and falls by up to 8 metres. Children often lose months of schooling because the nearest school is on land, too far by boat, or because the family has moved. In Kampong Luong, a 2026 survey found that only 41% of children aged 6–12 attended school regularly and that the average child missed 9 weeks per year.

## 2. The idea

Build three floating classroom boats that move with the villages. Each boat holds a classroom for 40 pupils, a small library, a toilet and a solar-powered charging point for tablets. Classes follow the national primary curriculum, and the boats are staffed by teachers recruited from the lake communities themselves.

## 3. Main activities

1. Design and build 3 floating classrooms with a local boat-builder (steel pontoon, timber superstructure, solar panels).
2. Recruit and train 9 teachers (3 per boat) from the lake communities, in partnership with the Provincial Teacher Training Centre.
3. Provide 120 tablets with offline lessons in Khmer for grades 1–6, plus printed books.
4. Organise "school boats" that collect pupils whose homes are far from the classroom.
5. Run evening literacy classes for parents twice a week.
6. Work with the Provincial Offices of Education to register the floating classrooms as official annexes of the nearest land school, so that pupils receive recognised certificates.

## 4. Expected results

- 360 children attend school regularly throughout the year (at least 80% attendance).
- Grade 6 completion among participating children rises above 85% (currently about 50%).
- 9 trained teachers employed by the Ministry of Education by the end of the project.
- 200 parents complete basic literacy classes.

## 6. Why Lake Learning Cambodia

Lake Learning Cambodia has run a floating library boat in Kampong Luong since 2021, reaching 600 children. The organization has an established relationship with both commune councils and the provincial education offices.

## 7. Risks and assumptions

- Boats must be well maintained; a maintenance budget and a trained caretaker per boat are included.
- Registration as annex schools requires ministry approval; discussions have started and the provincial offices are supportive.
- Storms on the lake in September–October may close the classrooms for short periods; the calendar will include catch-up weeks.
