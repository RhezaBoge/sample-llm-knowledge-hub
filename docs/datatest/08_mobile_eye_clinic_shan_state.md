# Mobile Eye Care Clinic, Southern Shan State

**Project Summary and Progress Update (to September 2025)**

- **Implementing organization:** Bright Sight Myanmar
- **Technical partner:** Taunggyi General Hospital, Department of Ophthalmology
- **Donor:** Vision for All Trust
- **Country:** Myanmar
- **Locations:** Taunggyi, Kalaw and Nyaungshwe townships, Southern Shan State
- **Period:** 1 May 2024 – 30 April 2026
- **Total budget:** USD 228,000

## 1. Why a mobile clinic

Uncorrected vision problems are among the most common disabilities in rural Shan State. Most people have never had an eye test, and the nearest eye department is in Taunggyi, a journey of several hours and a cost many families cannot afford. Many older people with cataracts have lost their sight although the condition is curable with a short operation.

## 2. How it works

A clinic team (one optometrist, two nurses, one driver and a community mobiliser) visits villages on a fixed monthly schedule published through village leaders and monasteries. On a clinic day the team:

1. Screens eyesight with simple charts.
2. Provides reading glasses and prescription glasses from a stock of standard lenses, fitted on the spot.
3. Identifies people with cataracts and books them for free surgery at Taunggyi General Hospital, with transport provided.
4. Refers other eye conditions to the hospital.

Village health volunteers trained by the project carry out basic screening between visits and keep a list of people who should see the clinic.

Post-surgery follow-up at one month shows that 94% of cataract patients regained vision good enough to read or work.

## 5. Budget

Spent to date: USD 154,000 (68%). Surgery costs per patient are lower than planned (USD 48 instead of USD 60) thanks to a bulk purchase of intraocular lenses.

## 6. Lessons

- The **predictable schedule** is the most valued feature: people plan to attend because they know when the clinic will return.
- Monasteries are effective partners for announcing clinic days.
- Transport to the hospital is the main barrier for surgery; providing it increased the share of booked patients who actually came from 55% to 88%.

## 7. Next steps

- Train 6 more village health volunteers in Kalaw.
- Discuss with the State Health Department how to continue the mobile clinic after the project with hospital staff.
