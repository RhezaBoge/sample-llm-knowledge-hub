# Digital Confidence for Seniors

**Programme Report 2025**

*Fictional example document*

**Organization:** Silver Connect Singapore
**Partners:** 8 community clubs (Toa Payoh Central, Toa Payoh East, Bedok, Kampong Chai Chee, Jurong West, Nanyang, Bukit Batok East, Yuhua); a local polytechnic (student volunteers); the Infocomm Media Development Authority (curriculum alignment)
**Funder:** Community Digital Inclusion Grant
**Country:** Singapore
**Location:** Toa Payoh, Bedok, Jurong West and Bukit Batok
**Period:** 1 January 2025 – 31 December 2026
**Total budget:** SGD 640,000
**Beneficiaries (2025):** 2,400 seniors aged 60 and above; 260 student volunteers
**Status:** Ongoing
**Programme director:** Ng Siew Lan
**SDGs:** SDG 4: Quality Education; SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities; SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

## 1. Summary

Small-group classes and one-to-one coaching help older residents use smartphones confidently and safely for messaging, video calls, online payments, transport apps and government services. Polytechnic students act as digital coaches, which also builds understanding between generations. In 2025, 2,400 seniors completed the basic course and 92% can now video-call family members on their own.

## 2. Why it matters

Although Singapore is highly connected, many residents over 60 use their phones only for calls. Government services, clinic appointments and payments are increasingly digital, and scams targeting seniors have risen. Confidence, not access to devices, is the main barrier.

## 3. Programme design

| Module | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Basic course | 4 sessions of 1.5 hours | Phone basics, messaging, photos, video calls, Wi-Fi |
| Safe online payments | 2 sessions | Mobile banking, QR payments, recognising scams |
| Government services | 2 sessions | Digital identity app, clinic appointments, transport apps |
| Drop-in help desk | weekly | Individual questions, device settings |

Classes are held in English, Mandarin, Malay and Tamil; printed guides with large text are provided.

## 4. Results in 2025

- 2,400 seniors completed the basic course (target 2,000); 1,100 continued to the safe payments module; 820 to the government services module.
- 260 student volunteers trained as digital coaches (each gives at least 20 hours).
- Weekly drop-in help desks run at all 8 community clubs; 6,300 visits recorded.
- 92% of participants say they can video-call family members on their own (survey, n=1,450).
- 78% now use a QR code or mobile payment at least monthly (14% before the course).
- A scam-awareness session was added after participants asked for it; 1,800 seniors attended, and 36 reported later that they recognised and avoided a scam attempt.
- 45 seniors trained as "peer coaches" who help at the drop-in desks.

## 5. Budget

Spent in 2025: SGD 305,000 (48%).

## 6. Lessons learned

- Groups of 6 or fewer work best; larger groups lose the shyest participants.
- Pairing the same student coach with the same seniors across all sessions builds trust.
- Peer coaches (seniors helping seniors) are highly effective and will be expanded.
- Many participants wanted to learn how to use health apps; a module is being developed with a polyclinic.

## 7. Plans for 2026

- Expand to three more neighbourhoods (Ang Mo Kio, Woodlands, Tampines).
- Add a health apps module and a Tamil-language peer coach group.
- Train 100 more peer coaches so that drop-in desks can continue after the grant ends.
- Final evaluation in November 2026.
